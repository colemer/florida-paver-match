Pavers in Fernandina Beach: what's different here

Fernandina Beach is unusually specific about pavers in its right-of-way, down to the paver standard and a notarized contractor certification. Downtown, Egans Creek and beachfront lots drain very differently.

  • Fernandina Beach allows pavers in city right-of-way for driveways and sidewalks if they meet FDOT Section 526 (ASTM C902 brick or C936 concrete pavers), with a notarized contractor certification before any final inspection.[4]
  • Fernandina Beach says it is not responsible for maintaining pavers in its right-of-way and that non-conforming sections must be removed and replaced within 48 hours of written notice.[4]

Storm and flooding history (drainage matters for pavers)

Paver base and joint sand depend on drainage. These are the recent storms that flooded or soaked this area.

  • Hurricane Matthew (October 7, 2016): Wind damage hit coastal locations including Fernandina Beach, with trees down on structures, and NWS issued a flash flood emergency for the Egans Creek drainage.[1]
  • Hurricane Irma (September 11, 2017): The Fernandina Beach tide gauge recorded a 6.34-foot peak storm tide (moderate flooding), 12.70 inches of rain fell, and trees fell onto homes.[2][3]

Flood risk and FEMA maps

Downtown sits on the Amelia River, Egans Creek drains the island's interior, and the beachfront faces the Atlantic, so flood risk varies block by block. Check each address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.[1][6]

Who issues permits in Fernandina Beach?

City of Fernandina Beach Building Department. Phone: 904-310-3135. Inside city limits, apply to the city (204 Ash St.; online or buildingpermits@fbfl.city). Amelia Island addresses outside the city are permitted by Nassau County.[5]

Rules for pavers differ by jurisdiction: some require a building permit for anything that adds impervious area, and work in the public right-of-way (most driveway aprons) usually needs a separate right-of-way or encroachment permit. Confirm with the office before work starts.
