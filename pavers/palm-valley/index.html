Pavers in Palm Valley: what's different here

Storm and flooding history (drainage matters for pavers)

Paver base and joint sand depend on drainage. These are the recent storms that flooded or soaked this area.

  • Hurricane Irma (September 11, 2017): Palm Valley residents on Roscoe Boulevard described their house as 'an island in the Intracoastal' after surge flooded yards and homes overnight; forecasts had suggested only 1 to 2 feet of surge.[1]
  • Hurricane Nicole (November 10, 2022): St. Johns County lists Roscoe Boulevard among the areas heavily impacted by Nicole's flooding, six weeks after Ian.[2]

Flood risk and FEMA maps

Palm Valley's risk is Intracoastal surge: water is pushed up the waterway and into backyards and homes on the low side of Roscoe Boulevard and nearby streets. Check each address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.[1][4]

Who issues permits in Palm Valley?

St. Johns County Building Services. Phone: 904-827-6800. Palm Valley is unincorporated; the county issues permits. Waterfront structures such as docks may need additional approvals.[3]

Rules for pavers differ by jurisdiction: some require a building permit for anything that adds impervious area, and work in the public right-of-way (most driveway aprons) usually needs a separate right-of-way or encroachment permit. Confirm with the office before work starts.

What does it cost?

Angi's 2026 data puts the U.S. average paver driveway at about $12,000, with most homeowners paying $6,000 to $18,000 (roughly $10 to $30 per square foot installed).[5] These are national figures; we have not found public local cost data. How Florida jobs differ.
