Pavers in Fruit Cove & St. Johns: what's different here

Fruit Cove and St. Johns paver projects range from driveways to riverfront patios, where the St. Johns River and Julington Creek have reached major flood levels. County right-of-way rules apply at the street.

  • In unincorporated St. Johns County, pavers are allowed in the county right-of-way for driveways but not as sidewalks, existing concrete sidewalks cannot be removed or replaced with pavers, and the driveway connection permit is $36.[4]
  • If your building permit already includes the driveway connection, St. Johns County does not require a separate right-of-way permit.[4]

Storm and flooding history (drainage matters for pavers)

Paver base and joint sand depend on drainage. These are the recent storms that flooded or soaked this area.

  • Hurricane Matthew (October 7, 2016): The Julington Creek gauge at Old St. Augustine Road reported a major-flooding storm tide of 4.61 feet NAVD88.[1]
  • Hurricane Irma (September 11, 2017): Irma set record levels along the St. Johns River, including 5.63 feet at the Buckman Bridge, as a nor'easter, high tides, surge and 7 to 11 inches of rain combined in the basin.[2]
  • Hurricane Ian (September 30, 2022): The St. Johns River gauge at the Shands Bridge peaked at 3.27 feet MHHW, above the 3-foot major-flood threshold.[3]

Flood risk and FEMA maps

Riverfront and creekfront lots along the St. Johns River and Julington Creek flood when the river backs up, and NOAA notes the basin drains slowly, so water can sit for days after a storm passes. Check the address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.[6][2][7]
