Paver Driveways in Florida

Last updated November 21, 2026 · Written for Florida homeowners by Florida Paver Match

Short answer: A paver driveway usually needs two approvals: anything your city requires for the work on your lot, and a right-of-way or encroachment permit for the apron between the property line and the street.[1][2]

Check whether your driveway apron sits in the public right-of-way and whether your city counts pavers toward impervious-surface limits. Both change what permits you need.

What to know

  • St. Johns County: pavers allowed in the right-of-way for driveways but not sidewalks; $36 driveway connection permit.
  • Jacksonville Beach: paver aprons go through a Revocable Encroachment Permit signed by the owner.
