Paver driveway permits in Northeast Florida: county and city rules (2026)

Last updated November 21, 2026 · Written for Florida homeowners by Florida Paver Match

Short answer

Short answer: Most paver driveways need a right-of-way or encroachment approval for the apron, and some cities also require a building permit when pavers add impervious area. St. Johns County charges $36 for a driveway connection permit and bans pavers on sidewalks; Jacksonville Beach uses an owner-signed Revocable Encroachment Permit; Clay County requires a driveway permit for paver upgrades that connect to county right-of-way.

  1. St. Johns County (unincorporated)
  2. Jacksonville Beach, Atlantic Beach and Neptune Beach
  3. Clay County, Orange Park and Fernandina Beach

St. Johns County (unincorporated)

Pavers are allowed in county right-of-way for driveways but not as sidewalks, and existing concrete sidewalks cannot be removed or replaced with pavers. The driveway connection permit is $36 per driveway; an approved building permit that includes the driveway connection does not need a separate right-of-way permit.[1]

Jacksonville Beach, Atlantic Beach and Neptune Beach

Jacksonville Beach: paver or specialty-concrete driveway aprons go through a Revocable Encroachment Permit, signed by the property owner only, with standard paver-driveway exhibits for lots with and without a curb.

Atlantic Beach: concrete slabs, pavers and patios that increase impervious area need a building permit, as do driveways.

Neptune Beach: driveways, decks, patios and walkways need permits, and work in the right-of-way (including pavers and driveway aprons) is permitted separately.[2][3][4]

Clay County, Orange Park and Fernandina Beach

Clay County requires a driveway permit for new, replacement, repair or paver upgrades connecting to county right-of-way (fees listed by the county: $175 new, $150 change or upgrade, $75 existing or exemption).

Town of Orange Park: work in the town right-of-way uses the Streets Division application.

Fernandina Beach: pavers are allowed in city right-of-way for driveways and sidewalks if they meet FDOT Section 526 (ASTM C902 brick or C936 concrete pavers); a notarized contractor certification is required before final inspection, and the city does not maintain pavers in its right-of-way.[5][6][7]

Frequently asked questions

Can I replace the sidewalk across my driveway with pavers?

Not in St. Johns County right-of-way: the county says existing concrete sidewalks cannot be removed or replaced with pavers. Fernandina Beach allows pavers for sidewalks in its right-of-way if they meet its specifications.[1][7]
